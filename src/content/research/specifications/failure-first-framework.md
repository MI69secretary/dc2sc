## The Failure-First Principle

Conventional market mechanisms measure success exclusively through transactional velocity, gross volume, and completed exchanges. The DC2SC research program rejects this bias.

Under the Failure-First Framework:
- **Null results are valid outcomes:** Discovering that participants cannot form a coherent collective position is a successful research result.
- **"Do not aggregate" is a legitimate resolution:** Choosing not to form a position preserves participant outside options and prevents coerced coordination.
- **Failure conditions are pre-registered:** Failure criteria are defined prior to empirical observation to prevent retrospective definition of success.

## Public Category-Level Failure Matrix

The research framework tracks coordination breakdowns across 15 public categories:

1. **Demand Formation:** Failure to aggregate coherent requirements across heterogeneous participants.
2. **Representation:** Unauthorized delegation or misalignment between representatives and participants.
3. **Ratification:** Inadequate consensus or opaque voting thresholds on proposed terms.
4. **Commitment:** Structural cost imbalances and lock-in risk upon position dissolution.
5. **Exit:** Friction, penalty, or inability to withdraw unilaterally before execution.
6. **Privacy:** Unintended exposure of individual constraint data or willingness-to-pay.
7. **Supplier Entry:** Inability to elicit competitive supplier responses to collective positions.
8. **Competition:** Collusive behavior, supplier boycott, or artificial market foreclosure.
9. **Quality:** Hidden quality degradation or non-conforming service delivery.
10. **Renegotiation:** Breakdown in post-offer terms adjustment or dispute resolution.
11. **Governance:** Inability to resolve participant conflicts or amend collective parameters.
12. **Measurement:** Distorted metrics that mistake volume or attention for durable bargaining power.
13. **Welfare:** Situations where participants achieve lower prices but suffer net welfare loss due to coordination drag.
14. **External Validity:** Failure of coordination patterns observed in stress tests to transfer to other domains.
15. **Legal Constraints:** Statutory, regulatory, or antitrust boundaries governing buyer coordination.
