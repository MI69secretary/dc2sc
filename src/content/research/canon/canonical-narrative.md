## Core Thesis

Modern digital markets excel at individualizing demand. People with shared constraints, overlapping requirements, and aligned preferences navigate markets as isolated transactional units. While digital interfaces provide unprecedented choice abundance, this abundance paradoxically coexists with a sharp contraction in collective bargaining agency.

DC2SC investigates whether independent individuals can form voluntary, intelligible, and participant-contestable market positions—without assuming that aggregation, lower prices, or increased transaction velocity are inherently beneficial.

## Canonical Distinctions

The canonical research framework rests on four constitutional distinctions:

1. **Similarity ≠ Authority:** Shared circumstances do not confer the right to represent others or commit on their behalf without explicit ratification.
2. **Ratification ≠ Commitment:** Approving the formulation of a collective market position is distinct from binding oneself to a financial or contractual obligation.
3. **Participant Contestability ≠ Supplier Contestability:** The capacity for participants to challenge, revise, or exit a collective position is non-negotiable and structurally distinct from supplier-side bidding.
4. **Transaction ≠ Success:** A completed transaction does not confirm that collective coordination produced superior outcomes to individual outside options. Null results and "do not aggregate" decisions are complete and valid research outcomes.

## Constitutional Sequence

A legitimate collective market position must transition through discrete, verifiable gates:

```text
Private intent
→ expressed constraints
→ negotiated common ground
→ proposed collective position
→ participant inspection / contestation / revision
→ representation validity gate
→ explicit ratification
→ conditional commitment
→ commitment validity gate
→ collective market position
→ supplier response, if any
→ offers under transparent terms
→ offer / decision validity gate
→ informed accept / reject / revise / exit
→ valid individual outside-option comparison
→ comparative performance evaluation
→ continue / redesign / stop / do not aggregate
```

## Non-Negotiable Invariants

- **Rights Floors Are Non-Compensable:** Material benefits (e.g. lower prices) cannot compensate for the erosion of participant privacy, exit rights, or contestability.
- **Outside Options Must Remain Accessible:** Participants retain the unilateral right to fall back to their individual alternatives at designated decision gates.
- **Failure-First Tracking:** Failure conditions, coordination costs, and preference divergences are tracked as primary research outputs.

## What the Canon Does Not Establish

- **No Field Validation:** Does not establish that collective buyer positions outperform individual market access in live commercial environments.
- **No Product Implementation:** Does not offer a live trading platform, booking engine, pooled discount mechanism, or consumer application.
- **No Universal Welfare Claims:** Does not claim aggregation is universally desirable or suitable for all market domains.

## Mandatory Research Boundary

> **Important Boundary Notice:**
> Frozen means constitutionally stable for the current research program. It does not mean empirically proven, field-validated, product-ready, or authorized for a live market.
